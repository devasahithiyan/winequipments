What is a closed circuit cooling tower? A closed circuit cooling tower (also known as an evaporative fluid cooler) isolates the primary process cooling water inside a closed loop of copper or stainless steel tubing coils. A secondary spray water system cascades over the outside of the coils while air is drawn upward by axial fans. This provides all the thermal heat-rejection benefits of evaporative cooling without ever allowing the process fluid to contact atmospheric dust, dirt, or bacteria, permanently preventing scaling inside induction furnace coils and laser cooling jackets.

Technical FAQ

Closed Circuit Cooling Tower Engineering Questions

How does a closed circuit cooling tower eliminate scaling?

The expensive process cooling fluid circulates inside closed, hermetically sealed copper or stainless steel heat exchanger coils. External recirculated water sprays over the outside of the coil while air is drawn across it. Because the internal process fluid never contacts the atmosphere, it never evaporates, accumulates dirt, or forms calcium scale.

When should I choose copper vs stainless steel coils?

High-conductivity copper coils offer maximum thermal heat transfer efficiency and smaller footprint for clean demineralized water or glycol loops. Stainless steel SS304/SS316 coils are selected for deionized water circuits, aggressive fluids, or corrosive chemical plant environments.
