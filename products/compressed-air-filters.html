What are the grades of compressed air filters? Industrial compressed air treatment utilizes a 4-stage filtration sequence: 1. Pre-Filter (Grade P - 3 to 5 micron): Removes bulk atmospheric dust, pipe rust, and water droplets. 2. General Purpose Filter (Grade G - 1 micron): Protects air dryers and refrigeration circuits. 3. Coalescing Sub-Micron Filter (Grade C - 0.01 micron): Removes 99.99% of microscopic oil aerosols and liquid mist. 4. Activated Carbon Filter (Grade V - 0.003 mg/m³): Adsorbs hydrocarbon oil vapors and odors for breathing air and pharmaceutical packaging.

Compressed Air Line Filter FAQs

Guidance on element micron ratings, replacement cycles, and pressure drops.

What is the correct installation order for multiple filter grades?

The standard sequence follows descending pore sizes: (1) Grade P (3–5 micron pre-filter) before air receivers/dryers; (2) Grade G (1 micron general purpose filter) at the dryer inlet; (3) Grade C (0.01 micron sub-micron coalescer) at the dryer outlet; and (4) Grade V (Activated carbon vapor filter) at point-of-use.

How often should filter cartridges be replaced?

Coalescing and particulate filter cartridges should be replaced every 6 to 12 months (or when differential pressure exceeds 0.35–0.5 bar / 5–7 psi). Saturated elements cause compressor power waste and risk element rupture.
