What is the purpose of an air receiver tank? An air receiver tank is a certified pressure vessel installed directly downstream of an air compressor. It performs three vital functions: (1) Acting as a buffer reservoir to supply high-flow peak air demands without compressor motor cycling; (2) Dampening reciprocating and screw compressor pressure pulsations to stabilize factory line pressure; and (3) Cooling discharged compressed air, precipitating up to 40% of bulk liquid water droplets before reaching downstream air dryers.

Engineering FAQ

Air Receiver Tank FAQs

Technical guidance on pressure vessel design, sizing, and statutory compliance.

What manufacturing standards are Win Equipments air receiver tanks built to?

All our pressure vessels are engineered in accordance with IS 2825 (Class I, II, or III) or ASME Section VIII Division 1. Each tank is supplied with mill test certificates (MTR) for plates and dished ends, plus an official 1.5x hydraulic test pressure certificate.

What safety fittings and accessories are included with each vessel?

Every vessel comes complete with an ASME/IBR certified brass safety relief valve calibrated to blow off at design pressure, a 4-inch dial pressure gauge, inspection hand-hole or man-hole, and a bottom condensate drain connection.

How do I calculate the recommended receiver tank size for my compressor?

A standard rule of thumb for industrial screw compressors is 3 to 4 Liters of receiver storage per CFM of compressor output (e.g. a 250 CFM compressor requires an 800L to 1,000L receiver tank). For high-demand cyclic pulses, larger 2,000L+ buffers are recommended.
