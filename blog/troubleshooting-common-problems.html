Troubleshooting Common Problems in Refrigeration Air Dryers

A quick diagnostic guide to fix water in lines, high dew points, and freezing issues.

Is your air dryer running but you still find water at the machine end? This is the most common complaint we receive. Before calling a service technician, run through this diagnostic checklist.

Problem 1: Water in the Lines vs. Water in the Dryer

First, verify where the water is coming from. Open the drain valve on your air receiver tank. If gallons of water pour out, your dryer (or the drain) isn't working.

Problem 2: Dryer is Running, but Dew Point is High

Possible Cause Action
Clogged Condenser Look at the condenser fins. If they are covered in dust, the refrigerant cannot cool down. Clean immediately with compressed air.
High Inlet Temp Is the air entering the dryer too hot (>55°C)? Check if the compressor's aftercooler fan is working.
Bypass Valve Open Ensure the 3-valve bypass assembly is set correctly. The middle valve should be CLOSED.

Problem 3: The Drain isn't Ejecting Water

Most failures are actually just Drain Valve Failures, not Dryer failures.

  1. Test It: Press the "Test" button on the timer.
  2. Listen: Do you hear a sharp "Hiss"? If yes, it's opening.
  3. Check Strainer: Close the isolation valve, open the strainer cap on the drain valve. Clean the mesh filter. It is likely blocked by rust particles.

Problem 4: Ice Formation on Pipes

If the copper pipes inside the dryer are frosted over, your Dew Point is too low (below 0°C), causing the condensate to freeze and block air flow.

Technician Note

This usually means the Hot Gas Bypass Valve needs adjustment or has failed. Do not attempt to adjust this yourself unless trained. Call Win Equipments service.

Problem 5: Dryer Won't Start

  • High Pressure Trip: Did the dryer overheat? Push the manual reset button on the High Pressure Switch.
  • Electrical: Check the contactor and overload relay. Ensure proper voltage (230V or 415V).
