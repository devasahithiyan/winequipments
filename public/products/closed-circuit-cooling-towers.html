    How it works

    The coil cooling tower works on heat transfer through a heat exchanger with extended fins, cooled by fan-driven air. The process water stays inside the coil.

    1. Hot water in

      Hot water from the diesel engine (or other load) enters the coil inlet header.

    2. Finned coil

      Water flows through copper tubes with extended fins; aerodynamically balanced axial fans push air across them.

    3. Cold water out

      Cooled water leaves the outlet header and a pump returns it to the engine to pick up heat again.

    Typical installation

    1. Diesel engine / process load
    2. Hot water to coil inlet
    3. WCC coil cooling tower
    4. Pump
    5. Cold water back to load

    Questions engineers ask

    How is a coil cooling tower different from an FRP cooling tower?

    In an FRP tower the water is sprayed into the air and cooled by evaporation, reaching wet bulb + 4 °C. In a WCC coil tower the water stays sealed inside finned copper tubes and is cooled by air alone, reaching ambient + 4 °C, with no water loss or contamination.

    Which genset sizes do WCC units suit?

    WCC coil cooling towers cover diesel gensets from 150 to 2000 kVA.

    What inlet water temperature can they handle?

    Inlet water from 75 to 90 °C at 2 to 3 bar g.
