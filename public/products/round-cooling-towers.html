    How it works

    A cooling tower is a heat-rejection device that extracts waste heat from the application to the atmosphere using a fan and motor. The heat rejection is evaporative.

    1. Distribute

      Hot water enters the main header and is distributed through the rotary sprinkler, whose jet recoil spins it for uniform flow.

    2. Film

      Water spreads over the honeycomb PVC fills as a film with a large contact surface.

    3. Evaporate

      The axial fan draws air up through the fills; a small part of the water evaporates and cools the rest.

    4. Collect

      Cooled water falls as droplets into the basin and is pumped back to the equipment.

    Questions engineers ask

    What outlet water temperature can a WCT tower achieve?

    Outlet water is rated at wet bulb temperature + 4 °C.

    Which fill should I choose?

    L (low-temperature) fills for inlet water up to 55 °C, H (high-temperature) fills for 55–85 °C, and polypropylene rings (P) for hot or dirty water up to above 90 °C.

    How much water is lost to evaporation?

    Evaporation loss ≈ 0.00085 × 1.8 × flow rate (m³/hr) × ΔT. For example, 30 m³/hr with a 5 °C range loses about 0.23 m³/hr.

    Where should the tower be installed?

    Install the tower so the pump sits below the sump outlet level; never place the pump above the sump outlet.
