    How it works

    Air drawn into a compressor carries moisture. Compression raises its temperature and its capacity to hold water vapour; when the air cools again, that vapour condenses.

    1. Store

      The receiver stores compressed air and evens out pressure swings between compressor and plant demand.

    2. Cool

      Air slows and cools in the vessel, so part of the condensate drops out downstream of the compressor.

    3. Drain

      Condensate collects at the bottom and is removed through the drain; the air leaving is still saturated and needs drying.

    Typical installation

    1. Air compressor
    2. Wet air receiver
    3. Refrigerated air dryer
    4. Zero-oil filter
    5. Dry air receiver

    Questions engineers ask

    What pressure ratings are available?

    Each standard WRV size is offered at 7, 10 or 12.5 bar g working pressure, with design pressures of 8, 11 and 14 bar g and hydrostatic test pressures of 12, 17 and 21 bar g.

    Can you build to ASME?

    Yes. Vessels can be fabricated to ASME VIII on requirement.

    Do I need a wet and a dry receiver?

    A typical installation uses a wet receiver after the compressor to settle condensate, and a dry receiver after the dryer and filters to buffer clean, dry air for the plant.
