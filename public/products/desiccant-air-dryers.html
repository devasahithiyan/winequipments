    Heatless Desiccant Air Dryers

    WHD Series

    Where a +3 °C dew point is not dry enough, WHD heatless dryers pass compressed air through a bed of activated alumina or molecular sieve. One tower dries while the other regenerates with a small flow of purge air, switching every few minutes.

    How it works

    A heatless desiccant dryer removes water vapour by adsorption onto a desiccant bed. No heater is used: a small part of the dried air regenerates the off-line tower.

    1. Pre-filter

      A pre-filter removes free water, oil and dust scale that would damage the desiccant.

    2. Adsorb

      Wet air flows up through the on-line tower; the desiccant bed adsorbs water vapour. Low velocities stop bed fluidisation and dusting.

    3. Purge

      A small flow of dry air is expanded through the off-line tower and exhausted through a muffler, stripping the moisture it holds.

    4. Switch

      The timer and solenoid valves switch towers on a fixed cycle so one tower is always drying.

    5. After-filter

      An after-filter removes desiccant dust before the air reaches your equipment.

    1. Air compressor

    Questions engineers ask

    Alumina or molecular sieve: which do I need?

    Activated alumina gives a −20 °C pressure dew point; molecular sieve gives −40 °C (both at 38 °C inlet and 7 bar g). Choose molecular sieve when your process needs the lower dew point.

    Why does a desiccant dryer need filters?

    A pre-filter keeps free water and dust off the desiccant, a zero-oil filter stops compressor oil from coating it, and an after-filter catches desiccant dust before it reaches your equipment.

    What does heatless mean?

    The off-line tower is regenerated with a small flow of dry purge air instead of a heater, so there is no heater to maintain.

    What working pressure is supported?

    Standard WHD dryers work from 7 to 12.5 bar g with a design pressure of 13.2 bar g. Higher pressures can be offered on request.
