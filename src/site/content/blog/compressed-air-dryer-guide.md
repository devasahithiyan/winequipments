A compressor squeezes moist air into a smaller volume and heats it. As it cools in the pipework the water condenses and reaches your machines. A compressed air dryer for your compressor removes that moisture before it does damage.

## What a dryer does

Air leaving the compressor is saturated with water vapour. A dryer lowers its **pressure dew point (PDP)**: the temperature at which water would begin to condense at line pressure. If your pipes never get colder than the PDP, liquid water does not form.

## The two main types

| | Refrigerated (WRD) | Heatless desiccant (WHD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Method | Chills the air, drains condensate | Adsorbs water vapour on a desiccant bed |
| Pressure dew point | +3 °C | −20 °C (alumina) or −40 °C (molecular sieve) |
| Capacity range | 10 to 2000 CFM | 300 to 2000 CFM |
| Inlet pressure | 3 to 16 bar g | 7 to 12.5 bar g |
| Inlet air temperature | 10 to 60 °C | 45 °C |
| Running cost | Refrigeration compressor and fan | Purge air taken from your compressed air |

**Refrigerated dryers:** the [WRD series](/products/refrigerated-air-dryers.html) pre-cools air in an air-to-air exchanger, chills it in the evaporator, separates the condensed water, then re-heats the air. Pressure drop is 0.2 bar g or less.

**Desiccant dryers:** the [WHD series](/products/desiccant-air-dryers.html) has twin towers. One dries while the other is regenerated with a small flow of dry purge air, switching on a 10-minute cycle. Dew point is rated at 38 °C inlet and 7 bar g.

For a full comparison read [refrigerated vs desiccant air dryers](/blog/refrigerated-vs-desiccant.html).

## ISO 8573-1 humidity classes

Specifications often call for a class instead of a dew point. Class 4 is a PDP of +3 °C or lower, class 3 is −20 °C or lower and class 2 is −40 °C or lower. See [ISO 8573-1 purity classes](/blog/iso-8573-1-compressed-air-purity-classes.html).

## Typical applications

The catalogue lists pneumatic tools, CNC machines, textile machinery, packaging and general automation as typical uses of refrigerated dryers. Desiccant dryers are listed for instrument air, pharmaceutical and electronics work, food packaging and paint lines. ## Where the dryer sits

Order matters. A typical sequence is compressor, aftercooler with moisture separator, receiver, pre-filter, dryer, after-filter. The aftercooler removes most of the water first, so the dryer handles less. See [aftercooler vs air dryer](/blog/aftercooler-vs-air-dryer.html). A desiccant dryer also needs a pre-filter and zero-oil filter, because oil damages the bed. See [compressed air filters](/products/compressed-air-filters.html).

## How to choose

1. **Required dew point:** +3 °C suits most plant air. Choose desiccant when pipes run below +3 °C or the process needs drier air.
2. **Actual flow:** use free air delivery in CFM, not the motor rating.
3. **Site conditions:** inlet temperature, ambient temperature and pressure.
4. **Future growth:** include any planned compressor.

## Sizing correction

Dryers are rated at fixed conditions. WRD dryers are rated at 45 °C inlet, 35 °C ambient, 7 bar g and +3 °C PDP, where all factors equal 1.0. At other conditions:

Dryer nominal capacity = compressor actual flow ÷ (C1 × C2 × C3 × C4)

C1 to C4 are the catalogue factors for inlet temperature, ambient temperature, pressure and dew point. For example the ambient factor is 0.91 at 40 °C and 0.87 at 45 °C, so the dryer must be rated higher than the compressor flow in a hot plant. The [air dryer sizing calculator](/engineering-tools/air-dryer-sizing.html) applies all four factors and picks the WRD or WHD model.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a dryer if I already have an aftercooler?
Usually yes. An aftercooler leaves the air saturated at a lower temperature, so water can still condense later in the lines.

### Which dryer for a screw compressor?
The type depends on your required dew point, not the compressor kind. Size from the compressor's actual flow and site conditions.

### Is a desiccant dryer always drier than a refrigerated one?
Yes. A WHD gives −20 °C or −40 °C against +3 °C, but it uses purge air, so choose it only where the dryness is needed.
