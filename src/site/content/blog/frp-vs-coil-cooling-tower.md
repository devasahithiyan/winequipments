Both an FRP cooling tower and a coil cooling tower take hot water from your equipment and send it back cooler. They do it in opposite ways. An FRP tower sprays the water into the air and lets a small part of it evaporate. A coil tower keeps the water sealed inside finned tubes and blows air across the outside. That one difference decides how cold the water can get, how much water you lose and which duties each one suits.

## How each one cools

**FRP tower (WCT round or square).** Hot water is distributed over honeycomb PVC fills by a rotary sprinkler (round) or spray nozzles (square). An axial fan draws air through the fills. A small fraction of the water evaporates, and the heat needed to evaporate it comes from the rest of the water, which cools. Because evaporation can cool water below the air temperature, the outlet is rated at **wet bulb + 4 °C**.

**Coil tower (WCC).** Hot water flows through copper tubes with extended fins, between heavy-wall steel headers. Axial fans push air across the fins. There is no evaporation, so the water can only approach the temperature of the air itself: the outlet is rated at **ambient + 4 °C**.

## Side by side

| | FRP cooling tower (WCT) | Coil cooling tower (WCC) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loop | Open: water sprayed into the air | Sealed: water stays in the coil |
| Cooling method | Evaporation over PVC fills | Air across finned copper tubes |
| Cold water | Wet bulb + 4 °C | Ambient + 4 °C |
| Inlet water | 40 to 100 °C (fill type by temperature) | 75 to 90 °C |
| Working pressure | 2 to 3 bar g | 2 to 3 bar g inlet, 0.5 bar max. pressure drop |
| Water loss | Evaporation, drift and blow-down | None from the coil |
| Water quality | Exposed to dust and air | Kept clean inside the coil |
| Capacity | 10 to 300 TR | 40 to 280 TR |
| Typical duty | Open process water, condenser water | Diesel genset jacket water (150 to 2000 kVA), closed loops |

## Cold water temperature

The wet bulb temperature is always at or below the dry bulb (ambient) temperature, and the gap is widest in dry weather. So for the same day, an FRP tower can deliver colder water than a coil tower.

As an illustration only: on a day with 34 °C ambient and 26 °C wet bulb, an FRP tower would be rated to give about 30 °C water, and a coil tower about 38 °C. Use your own site's design dry bulb and wet bulb values. If your process needs water near or below ambient, an FRP tower (or a [chiller](/products/industrial-process-chillers.html)) is the only one of the two that can do it.

A coil tower makes sense when the water is coming back very hot and only needs to be brought down to a safe working temperature, as with engine jacket water returning at 75 to 90 °C.

## Water loss

An FRP tower uses water as it works. The catalogue formula is:

**Evaporation loss = 0.00085 × 1.8 × flow (m³/hr) × ΔT**

A tower circulating 30 m³/hr with a 5 °C range evaporates about 0.23 m³/hr, which is around 5.5 m³ over a 24-hour day (calculated). Drift and blow-down come on top. Square towers fit PVC drift eliminators (600 × 600 × 130 mm) and round towers use a rotary sprinkler designed to minimise drift, but some make-up water is always needed.

A coil tower has no evaporation and no drift from the process loop. Once filled, the circuit only needs topping up for leaks.

## Water quality and the sealed loop

In an open tower, the water picks up dust from the air, and evaporation concentrates dissolved salts, so it needs blow-down and treatment. That is fine for many process circuits. It is not fine for a diesel engine's jacket water, which must stay clean and treated to protect the engine. A WCC keeps that water sealed, so nothing from the air gets in.

## Fill choice on FRP towers

FRP towers handle hot water by choosing the right fill: L fills for inlet water up to 55 °C, H fills for 55 to 85 °C, and polypropylene rings (P) for hot or dirty water, as they withstand more than 90 °C. All three are offered on every round and square model.

## Choose an FRP tower when

- You need the coldest possible water, close to wet bulb
- The circuit is an open process loop, or a chiller or compressor condenser loop
- Make-up water is available and you have a treatment and blow-down routine
- The load is anywhere from 10 to 300 TR

## Choose a coil tower when

- The water must stay sealed and clean, such as diesel genset jacket water
- Hot water returns at 75 to 90 °C and ambient + 4 °C is cold enough
- Water is scarce, or you want no make-up for the process loop
- The genset is in the 150 to 2000 kVA range (WCC 40 to 280 TR)

## Frequently asked questions

### Which cooling tower gives colder water, FRP or coil?
An FRP tower. It is rated at wet bulb + 4 °C, and wet bulb is always at or below ambient, while a coil tower is rated at ambient + 4 °C.

### Does a coil cooling tower lose water?
No. The water stays sealed in the coil and there is no evaporation or drift from the process loop. Once filled it only needs topping up for leaks.

### How much water does an FRP cooling tower evaporate?
Evaporation loss = 0.00085 × 1.8 × flow (m³/hr) × range. At 30 m³/hr and a 5 °C range that is about 0.23 m³/hr, or around 5.5 m³ a day (calculated), plus drift and blow-down.

### When should I choose a coil cooling tower?
When the water must stay sealed and clean, such as diesel genset jacket water returning at 75 to 90 °C, when ambient + 4 °C is cold enough, or when make-up water is scarce. WCC models cover 40 to 280 TR for 150 to 2000 kVA gensets.
