Compressed air carries water. When it cools in the receiver, filters and pipework, that water condenses, often mixed with compressor oil. If it is not removed it is carried to your tools and machines. An automatic drain valve removes it without an operator opening a manual cock.

## Why not use a manual drain

A manual drain depends on someone remembering, on every shift, at every low point. Left open, it also wastes compressed air. An automatic drain discharges the condensate on a schedule or by level, so the system stays clear without shutting down.

## Types and how each works

| Type | How it works | Air loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electronic timer | Opens on an adjustable interval for a set time | Some air escapes while open |
| Micro-controller | Same idea with finer interval and discharge settings | Some air escapes while open |
| Float / no air loss | The condensate level operates the valve, so it discharges condensate only | None |

For the WDV range, the catalogue gives these settings:

- **Electronic timer (WDV M12, H12, L40, H40, B16, ZA16):** interval 1 to 120 min (136 min on the H models), discharge time 4 or 8 s depending on the model.
- **Micro-controller (WDV E16):** interval 0.5 to 45 min and discharge 0.5 to 10 s, brass body with a built-in strainer.
- **No air loss (WDV F16):** discharges condensate only and needs no electrical supply. The catalogue lists a mechanical float type that also needs no power.

A strainer-cum-manual shut-off valve ahead of the drain protects it and lets you isolate it for service.

## Where to fit one

The catalogue lists these drain points:

- Air receiver drain
- Compressed air filter drain
- Refrigerated dryer drain
- Pipeline drop leg
- Moisture separator drain

Each is a low point where water collects. See the [air receiver tanks](/products/air-receiver-tanks.html), [compressed air filters](/products/compressed-air-filters.html) and [refrigerated air dryers](/products/refrigerated-air-dryers.html) that these drains serve.

Then check:

1. **Pressure:** standard is 7 to 16 kgf/cm², with 40 and 70 optional.
2. **Condensate temperature:** up to 90 °C.
3. **Supply:** 230 V AC standard; 24 V, 48 V, 110 V AC or DC to order. Protection is IP55 or IP65.
4. **Air loss:** if wasted air matters, consider the no-air-loss type.
5. **Material:** cast aluminium, stainless steel or brass depending on the model.

## Fitting tips

- Fit the strainer or shut-off valve before the drain so you can service it without depressurising the whole system.
- Pipe the discharge to a safe place. Compressor condensate contains oil and should not be run straight to a drain; treat it as your site rules require.
- Keep the drain at the lowest point of the vessel or pipe, so water actually reaches it.
- Check electrical supply and protection class (IP55 or IP65) against where the valve will sit, particularly outdoors.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should the valve open?
Set the interval and discharge time so the condensate clears without blowing air for long. Start from the model's range and adjust by watching how much water appears.

### Do I need a drain on every filter and dryer?
Yes. Each collects condensate at its own low point, so each needs its own drain.

### Can a drain valve block with dirt?
The catalogue describes high-velocity discharge without clogging, and a strainer protects the valve. Clean the strainer as part of routine maintenance.
