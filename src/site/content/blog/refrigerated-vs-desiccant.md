The question to ask is not "which dryer is better" but "how dry does my air need to be". That is set by the pressure dew point (PDP) your process can tolerate.

## The difference in one line

- A **refrigerated dryer** cools the air and drains the condensate. WRD dryers deliver a **+3 °C** pressure dew point.
- A **heatless desiccant dryer** adsorbs water vapour onto a desiccant bed. WHD dryers deliver **−20 °C** with activated alumina or **−40 °C** with molecular sieve (rated at 38 °C inlet and 7 bar g).

## Side by side

| | Refrigerated (WRD) | Heatless desiccant (WHD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it dries | Chills the air, condensate drained | Water vapour adsorbed on alumina or molecular sieve |
| Pressure dew point | +3 °C | −20 °C or −40 °C |
| ISO 8573-1 humidity class | Class 4 (≤ +3 °C) | Class 3 (≤ −20 °C) or class 2 (≤ −40 °C) |
| Regeneration | Not needed | A small flow of dry purge air regenerates the off-line tower |
| Standard range | 10 to 2000 CFM | 300 to 2000 CFM |
| Filters | Pre-filter recommended | Pre-filter and zero-oil filter before, after-filter for desiccant dust |
| Power | Refrigeration compressor and fan | Timer and solenoid valves only (no heater) |

## Choose a refrigerated dryer when

Your air lines stay above +3 °C and the process only needs liquid water kept out. That covers most plant air: pneumatic tools and cylinders, CNC machines, textile machinery, packaging and general automation.

## Choose a desiccant dryer when

- Air lines run outdoors or through cold areas where pipework can drop below +3 °C.
- The process needs very dry air, for example instrument air, pharmaceutical or electronics work, food packaging or paint lines, which our catalogue lists as typical WHD applications.
- A customer or standard specifies ISO 8573-1 humidity class 3 or better.

## How a heatless dryer works

Wet air flows up through the on-line tower and the desiccant adsorbs the water vapour. Meanwhile a small flow of dried air is expanded through the off-line tower and vented through a muffler, stripping the moisture from that bed. A timer switches the towers on a fixed cycle (10 minutes on WHD dryers) so one tower is always drying.

Because the purge air comes from your compressed air, a desiccant dryer has a running cost a refrigerated dryer does not. That is why it makes sense only where the lower dew point is actually needed.

## Filters matter more with desiccant

Oil and liquid water ruin desiccant. Fit a pre-filter to remove free water and dust, a zero-oil filter so compressor oil cannot coat the bed, and an after-filter to catch desiccant dust. See our [compressed air filters](/products/compressed-air-filters.html) for the grades.
