Two quotations for "a 100 CFM dryer" can be very different machines. Before comparing prices, make sure they are sized and specified for the same job. These are the factors that set the cost.

## 1. Capacity at your conditions, not the catalogue rating

Dryer capacity falls in hot plants. A dryer rated 250 CFM at 35 °C ambient delivers less at 40 °C. If one supplier has corrected for your conditions and another has not, the cheaper quote may simply be an undersized dryer. Use the [sizing calculator](/engineering-tools/air-dryer-sizing.html) to check.

## 2. Dew point

A refrigerated dryer at +3 °C costs far less to buy and run than a desiccant dryer at −20 °C or −40 °C. Buy the dew point your process needs; see [refrigerated vs desiccant](/blog/refrigerated-vs-desiccant.html).

## 3. Heat exchanger and refrigeration parts

Ask what the heat exchanger is made of and how it is built, which refrigeration compressor is used, and whether there is a hot gas bypass valve to stop freezing at low load. WRD dryers use a copper tube-in-tube co-axial heat exchanger, a hermetic reciprocating or scroll compressor, a thermostatic expansion valve and a hot gas bypass valve.

## 4. Pressure drop

Every bar of pressure drop has to be made up by the compressor. Ask for the rated pressure drop; WRD dryers are rated at 0.2 bar g or less.

## 5. Controls and drains

A controller that shows dew point and alarms, and an automatic drain, are part of what you are paying for. Check what is included.

## 6. Filters and installation

Pre-filters, after-filters, drains, a bypass, installation and commissioning may or may not be in the price.

## Questions to ask every supplier

- At what inlet temperature, ambient, pressure and dew point is the capacity quoted?
- What is the pressure drop at rated flow?
- What heat exchanger, refrigeration compressor and refrigerant are used?
- What is included: filters, drains, bypass, installation, commissioning?
- Who services it, and where do spares come from?
