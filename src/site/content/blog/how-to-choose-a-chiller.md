A chiller is bought on three numbers: how much heat it must remove, the water temperature it must reach, and the air or water it can reject heat to. Get those right and the model follows.

## 1. Work out the heat load

The heat load is mass flow times the temperature drop across the process: Q = m × ΔT, where m is the water flow in litres per minute and ΔT is the inlet minus the outlet temperature. In TR, divide the litres per minute times the temperature rise by 50.4. The [chiller tonnage calculator](/engineering-tools/chiller-tonnage-calculator.html) does this sum and the correction below.

## 2. Correct for the outlet temperature

Our WCP chillers are rated at a 15 °C water outlet. Colder water takes more of the refrigeration, so the chiller must be larger for the same heat load.

| Water outlet temperature | Correction factor |
|---|---|
| 5 °C | 0.6 |
| 10 °C | 0.75 |
| 15 °C | 1.0 |
| 20 °C | 1.16 |
| 25 °C | 1.3 |

## 3. Correct for the ambient temperature

WCP chillers are rated at a 40 °C ambient. A hotter site needs a larger chiller.

| Ambient temperature | Correction factor |
|---|---|
| 30 °C | 1.2 |
| 35 °C | 1.1 |
| 40 °C | 1.0 |
| 45 °C | 0.9 |
| 50 °C | 0.8 |

The nominal chiller capacity is the heat load divided by the outlet factor times the ambient factor.

**Worked example (calculated):** a 10 TR heat load, a 10 °C outlet (0.75) and a 45 °C ambient (0.9). Nominal capacity = 10 ÷ (0.75 × 0.9) = 14.8 TR, so a 15 TR unit is the next size up.

## 4. Choose air-cooled or water-cooled

- **Air-cooled** units reject heat to the air, so they need no cooling tower. They suit sites where water is scarce or hard, or where there is no space for a tower. Our packaged air-cooled range runs from 0.5 to 15 TR.
- **Water-cooled** units reject heat to a cooling tower. On hot sites a water-cooled chiller with a tower can use less power.

The [air-cooled versus water-cooled guide](/blog/air-cooled-vs-water-cooled-chiller.html) covers the trade-off in more detail.

## 5. Check the water side

- **Inlet pressure:** 1 bar g is ideal; the maximum is 2 to 4 bar g.
- **Tank:** the WCP tank is closed and insulated. Up to WCP 075 it is stainless steel; WCP 100 to 200 come in stainless or mild steel.
- **Pump:** the WCP chillers have a built-in circulating pump.
- **Water inlet range:** 5 to 30 °C.

## 6. What to send us for a quote

1. Water flow in litres per minute, or the heat load in TR.
2. The water outlet temperature you need.
3. The site ambient temperature.
4. Air-cooled or water-cooled, and the voltage and phase.
5. Whether you need a tank and pump.
