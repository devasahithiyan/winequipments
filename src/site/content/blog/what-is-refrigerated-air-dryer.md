Every air compressor is also a water pump. The air it draws in carries water vapour, and compression concentrates it. When that hot, saturated air cools in the receiver and pipework, the vapour condenses into liquid water that reaches your valves, cylinders, tools and product.

A refrigerated air dryer removes that water before it gets there, by cooling the air until the moisture condenses and can be drained away.

## How much water are we talking about?

Air leaving a compressor's aftercooler is typically saturated. At 7 bar g and 45 °C, saturated air holds about 7.5 g of water vapour per kg of air. Cooled to a +3 °C pressure dew point, it can hold only about 0.6 g/kg, so roughly 92% of the vapour has to condense.

For a 100 CFM compressor running continuously, that works out to about 34 litres of water a day (calculated for saturated air at 45 °C and 7 bar g dried to +3 °C). Larger compressors produce proportionally more.

## The working cycle

A WRD refrigerated dryer works in five stages:

1. **Pre-cool.** Hot, saturated air enters the air-to-air section of the heat exchanger, where the outgoing cold air pre-cools it. This reduces the load on the refrigeration system.
2. **Chill.** In the evaporator the air is cooled to the required dew point by refrigerant supplied by the refrigeration compressor and condenser.
3. **Separate.** Cold air can hold far less water vapour, so the moisture condenses. A water separator collects it and an automatic drain valve discharges it.
4. **Re-heat.** The cold, dry air passes back through the air-to-air exchanger and warms up before it leaves the dryer, so pipes downstream do not sweat.
5. **Match the load.** A thermostatic expansion valve and a hot gas bypass valve match the refrigeration system to the air load, holding the dew point steady when demand changes.

## What "pressure dew point" means

The pressure dew point (PDP) is the temperature at which water starts to condense from the compressed air at line pressure. A +3 °C PDP means liquid water cannot form as long as your pipework stays above +3 °C. In most Indian plants that is the case all year round.

## Inside a WRD dryer

| Component | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Co-axial heat exchanger | Copper tube-in-tube coil that acts as pre-cooler and evaporator, insulated with PUF |
| Refrigeration compressor | Hermetically sealed reciprocating or scroll compressor |
| Thermostatic expansion valve | Holds a constant dew point as the load varies |
| Hot gas bypass valve | Keeps the evaporator above 5 °C so condensate cannot freeze |
| Temperature controller | Shows dew point, inlet and condensing temperatures, with alarms and a compressor on-delay |
| Condenser | Air-cooled plate-fin exchanger; water-cooled option on larger models |

WRD dryers are rated per ISO 7183 at 45 °C inlet, 35 °C ambient and 7 bar g, with a pressure drop of 0.2 bar g or less.

## When a refrigerated dryer is the right choice

A +3 °C dew point suits most plant air: pneumatic tools and cylinders, CNC machines, textile machinery, packaging, general automation. Our catalogue lists automobile, cement, chemical, electronics, food processing, foundry, glass, leather, textile, paper, rice mills, power plants and printing among typical users.
