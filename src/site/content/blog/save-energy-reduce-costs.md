Compressed air is one of the most expensive utilities in a plant, because most of the compressor's power ends up as heat. Water in that air adds further cost in ways that are easy to overlook.

## Corrosion and leaks

Liquid water rusts steel pipework and receivers. Corroded pipes leak, and every leak is compressed air the compressor has already paid to make. Rust and scale also travel downstream and block filters, drains and valves.

## Pressure drop

Blocked filters, scaled pipes and undersized treatment equipment all add pressure drop, so the compressor must run at a higher pressure to deliver the same pressure at the machines. That takes more power; a commonly used rule of thumb is roughly 7% more compressor power for each extra bar.

When choosing treatment equipment, check its rated pressure drop. WRD refrigerated dryers are rated at 0.2 bar g or less, and WMF filters list their clean pressure drop by grade (from 0.03 bar g for GP to 0.12 bar g for ZC). Change filter elements at 0.7 bar g differential rather than leaving them to block.

## Wear on valves, cylinders and tools

Water washes lubricant out of pneumatic components and corrodes them, so seals, valves and cylinders are replaced more often.

## Rejects and rework

Where air touches the product, water appears as defects: marks on fabric, spots in paint, wet parts, moisture in packaging.

## Drains that waste air

Condensate must be drained, but a drain left cracked open, or a timer drain set to open too long, vents compressed air continuously. Use automatic drains matched to the duty; a no-air-loss drain such as the WDV F16 discharges only condensate.

## Getting the treatment right

1. Cool the air after the compressor with an [aftercooler](/products/industrial-aftercoolers.html) so most of the water condenses early.
2. Drain the receiver and every filter automatically.
3. Dry the air with a correctly sized [refrigerated dryer](/products/refrigerated-air-dryers.html), or a desiccant dryer where the process needs it.
4. Filter to the ISO 8573-1 class your process needs, and no tighter.
