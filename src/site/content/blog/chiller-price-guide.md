Price rises with capacity, but not in a straight line. The refrigeration compressor, condenser and controls are a large share of a small chiller, so a 1 TR unit costs more per TR than a 10 TR unit. See [how to size a process chiller](/blog/how-to-size-a-process-chiller.html) before you compare quotes, because an undersized chiller is always the cheapest one.

## What changes the price

- **Capacity at your conditions.** Catalogue ratings assume standard conditions. Hot plants, high ambient temperature or a colder outlet need a larger unit.
- **Cooling type.** Air-cooled and water-cooled units need different condensers and site arrangements.
- **Materials.** Stainless steel or cast iron instead of mild steel, copper coils, or a special refrigerant change the price.
- **What is included.** Pumps, tanks, filters, controls, installation and commissioning may or may not be part of the quote.
- **Taxes and freight.** Check whether GST, packing and delivery to your site are included.

## How to get an exact price

Send us these details and our engineers will reply with a sized model and a quote:

1. Heat load in TR, or water flow in litres per minute and the temperature rise.
2. The water outlet temperature you need.
3. The site ambient temperature.
4. Air-cooled or water-cooled, and the voltage and phase.
5. Whether you need a tank and pump.

The [chiller tonnage calculator](/engineering-tools/chiller-tonnage-calculator.html) works out the TR from the first two values. The [guide to choosing a chiller](/blog/how-to-choose-a-chiller.html) explains the other choices.
