A cooling tower removes heat from process water and rejects it to the atmosphere. Towers are grouped in several ways: by how air moves, by whether the water touches the air, and by shape. This guide covers the types Win Equipments builds first, then the general types you may see in other literature.

## The types we build

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Round (bottle) FRP towers

[Round cooling towers](/products/round-cooling-towers.html) spray water through a rotary sprinkler, spun by jet recoil, over honeycomb PVC fills. An axial fan draws air through and a small part of the water evaporates, cooling the rest. The casing is UV-resin FRP on a hot-dip galvanised structure.

### Square FRP towers

[Square cooling towers](/products/square-cooling-towers.html) use the same evaporative principle in a square footprint that fits against walls and in rows. Water is distributed by spray nozzles, and PVC drift eliminators reduce carry-over. See [round vs square cooling towers](/blog/round-vs-square-cooling-towers.html) for the comparison.

### Coil cooling towers (dry coolers)

[Coil cooling towers](/products/closed-circuit-cooling-towers.html) pass hot water through copper tubes with extended fins while axial fans push air across them. The water never touches the air, so it stays clean and does not evaporate. The catalogue lists them for diesel gensets from 150 to 2000 kVA and closed process loops. Read [coil cooling towers for diesel gensets](/blog/coil-cooling-towers-for-diesel-gensets.html).

The trade-off is temperature: an evaporative tower can reach wet bulb + 4 °C, while a dry coil is limited to ambient + 4 °C. See also [FRP vs coil cooling towers](/blog/frp-vs-coil-cooling-tower.html).

## Other types in general (not made by Win)

The following is general engineering knowledge. We do not manufacture these.

- **Natural draft towers:** very tall hyperbolic towers that move air by buoyancy, used at large power stations.
- **Mechanical draft towers:** use a fan, either **induced draft** (fan pulls air through) or **forced draft** (fan pushes air in). The towers above are mechanical draft.
- **Counter flow and cross flow:** describe the air direction relative to the water. See [cross flow vs counter flow](/blog/cross-flow-vs-counter-flow-cooling-tower.html).
- **Open and closed circuit:** open towers expose the water to air, closed circuit towers keep the process fluid inside a coil.

## How to choose

1. **Does the water need to stay sealed?** If yes (engine jacket water, clean closed loops), consider a coil tower.
2. **How cold must the water be?** Compare your required temperature with the site wet bulb (evaporative) or ambient (dry).
3. **How much heat?** Work out TR from flow and temperature rise. The [cooling tower calculator](/engineering-tools/cooling-tower-calculator.html) picks a WCT model.
4. **What space is available?** Round and square towers suit different layouts.
5. **What water quality do you have?** It sets the fill type and blowdown plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which cooling tower type is cheapest to run?
It depends on the duty. Evaporative towers use water through evaporation; dry coil towers do not, but they cool only to ambient + 4 °C. Compare on your actual load and site.

### Can a cooling tower serve a chiller?
Yes. A water-cooled chiller rejects its condenser heat to a tower. See [air-cooled vs water-cooled chillers](/blog/air-cooled-vs-water-cooled-chiller.html).

Our catalogue describes WCT towers with an axial fan drawing air through the fills. For the exact air path of a given model, ask us.
