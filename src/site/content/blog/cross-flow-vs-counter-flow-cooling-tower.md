Every evaporative cooling tower brings hot water and air into contact so that a small part of the water evaporates and cools the rest. "Cross flow" and "counter flow" describe only one thing: the direction the air moves relative to the falling water.

## What the two terms mean

- **Counter flow:** water falls down through the fill and air moves up through it, in the opposite direction. The air passes through the full depth of the fill from the bottom.
- **Cross flow:** water falls down through the fill and air moves horizontally across it, at right angles. Air enters through the sides of the fill and leaves towards a central plenum, usually under the fan.

This is general engineering knowledge, not a statement about any one manufacturer's product.

## Side by side

| | Counter flow | Cross flow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air direction | Upward, against the water | Horizontal, across the water |
| Air inlet | Below the fill | Along the side of the fill |
| Water distribution | Usually pressurised spray or rotary sprinkler above the fill | Often gravity-fed from a distribution basin above the fill |
| Footprint | Smaller plan area for a given duty is common | Larger plan area, lower overall height is common |
| Pump head | Includes spray pressure | Can be lower where gravity distribution is used |
| Basin and fill access | Fill is enclosed above the basin | Fill is often easier to see and reach from the side |

The last three rows are tendencies, not rules. A well-designed tower of either type can be built to suit a site, and the fill, fan and water loading matter more to performance than the label alone.

## What matters more than the label

For a given duty, the cold water temperature depends on the same few things in either arrangement:

- The **wet bulb temperature** at your site. Cold water can never fall below it.
- The **approach** (cold water minus wet bulb) you ask for. A small approach needs a larger tower.
- The **range** (hot water minus cold water) and the water flow.
- The fill type and the air volume the fan delivers.

## How to choose

1. Work out heat load, water flow and range. The [cooling tower calculator](/engineering-tools/cooling-tower-calculator.html) does this and picks a WCT model.
2. Note your hottest wet bulb, not the average.
3. Decide the space you have: plan area against height.
4. Ask how you will reach the fill, sprinklers or nozzles for cleaning.
5. Confirm the water quality, because that decides the fill type.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is counter flow more efficient than cross flow?
Not as a rule. Efficiency depends on the fill, air volume and water loading. Counter flow is often more compact, while cross flow often offers easier access. Compare actual rated duty at your wet bulb.

### Does the airflow direction change the cold water temperature?
Only through how the specific tower is designed. The cold water cannot go below the wet bulb temperature in either arrangement.

### Which type is a Win round tower?
The catalogue describes air drawn up through the fills against falling water, which is a counter flow arrangement.
