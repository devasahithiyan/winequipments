Three temperatures decide what a cooling tower can do. Once you know them, sizing is straightforward.

## The three temperatures

- **Range** is how much the tower cools the water: hot water in minus cold water out.
- **Wet bulb** is the lowest temperature evaporation can reach in your local air. It depends on the weather, not on the tower.
- **Approach** is how close the cold water gets to the wet bulb: cold water out minus wet bulb.

A tower can never cool water below the wet bulb. WCT towers are rated to deliver cold water at **wet bulb + 4 °C**, an approach of 4 °C.

## Heat load in TR

> Heat load (TR) = flow (m³/hr) × range (°C) × 1,000 ÷ 3,024

WCT towers are rated for **0.6 m³/hr of water per TR**, which is a 5 °C range. For example, 30 m³/hr cooled by 5 °C is 30 × 5 × 1,000 ÷ 3,024 = **49.6 TR**, which matches the WCT 050 (50 TR, 30 m³/hr).

## Choose the fill for your hot water

| Hot water temperature | Fill type |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 55 °C | L: low-temperature PVC fills |
| 55 to 85 °C | H: high-temperature fills |
| Hotter or dirty water | P: polypropylene rings, which withstand more than 90 °C |

## Evaporation loss

A tower cools by evaporating a small part of the water, so you must top it up. From our catalogue:

> Evaporation loss = 0.00085 × 1.8 × flow (m³/hr) × range (°C)

For 30 m³/hr and a 5 °C range that is about **0.23 m³/hr**. Drift and blow-down add to the make-up water you need.

## Installation note

Install the tower so the pump sits below the sump outlet level. A pump above the sump outlet can lose prime.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is cooling tower approach?
Approach is how close the cold water gets to the wet bulb temperature: cold water out minus wet bulb. WCT towers are rated for an approach of 4 °C.

### What is cooling tower range?
Range is how much the tower cools the water: hot water in minus cold water out. WCT towers are rated for 0.6 m³/hr of water per TR, which is a 5 °C range.

### How do you calculate cooling tower capacity in TR?
Heat load (TR) = flow (m³/hr) × range (°C) × 1,000 ÷ 3,024. For example, 30 m³/hr cooled by 5 °C is 49.6 TR, which matches the WCT 050.

### How do you calculate cooling tower evaporation loss?
Evaporation loss = 0.00085 × 1.8 × flow (m³/hr) × range (°C). For 30 m³/hr and a 5 °C range that is about 0.23 m³/hr. Drift and blow-down add to the make-up water needed.

### Can a cooling tower cool water below the wet bulb temperature?
No. The wet bulb is the lowest temperature evaporation can reach in your local air, so it is the limit for any evaporative cooling tower.
