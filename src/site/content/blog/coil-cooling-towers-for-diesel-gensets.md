A diesel genset's engine jacket water has to stay clean and sealed. A coil cooling tower cools it inside a finned coil, so the water never touches the air.

## How it works

1. **Hot water in.** Hot jacket water from the engine enters the coil's inlet header.
2. **Finned coil.** The water flows through copper tubes with extended fins while axial fans push air across them.
3. **Cold water out.** Cooled water leaves the outlet header and the pump returns it to the engine.

Because cooling is by air alone, there is no evaporation, no water loss and no contamination of the circuit.

## Coil tower or FRP tower?

| | Coil cooling tower (WCC) | FRP cooling tower (WCT) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water contact with air | None: sealed in the coil | Sprayed into the air |
| Cooling method | Air across finned tubes | Evaporation |
| Cold water | Ambient + 4 °C | Wet bulb + 4 °C |
| Water loss | None | Evaporation, drift and blow-down |
| Typical duty | Genset jacket water, closed loops | Open process water circuits |

An FRP tower can reach colder water because evaporation cools below the air temperature. A coil tower suits loops where the water must stay sealed and hot water at 75 to 90 °C comes back from the engine.
