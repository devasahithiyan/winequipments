A refrigerated dryer rejects the heat it removes from the air through its condenser. Heat and dust are what make that hard, so in Indian plants the routine below matters most in summer.

## Every shift

- **Check the drain is discharging water.** Use the test button on the drain valve. If nothing comes out, the strainer or valve may be blocked; if water collects downstream, the drain is not keeping up.
- **Read the controller.** WRD dryers show dew point, inlet and condensing temperatures, with alarms. Note anything outside normal.
- **Check the inlet temperature.** WRD dryers accept 10 to 60 °C inlet air. Air arriving hotter usually means the compressor's aftercooler needs attention.

## Every week

- **Clean the condenser.** Dust, fibre and oil mist on the condenser fins are the most common cause of high condensing temperature and poor dew point. Blow clean with compressed air from the clean side outwards, without bending the fins.
- **Keep the air around the dryer cool.** The condenser needs fresh air. Do not let hot exhaust from the compressor recirculate to the dryer.

## Every month

- **Check the pre-filter differential.** Replace the element when the differential pressure reaches 0.7 bar g.
- **Clean drain strainers.** Rust and scale block them.
- **Listen to the condenser fan and refrigeration compressor** for any change in noise or vibration.

## Once a year (by a technician)

- Check refrigerant pressures and the refrigeration compressor current.
- Deep-clean the condenser.
- Check the hot gas bypass valve, which keeps the evaporator above 5 °C so condensate cannot freeze.
- Check electrical connections, contactors and overload settings.

## Watch the bypass valves

Most dryers are installed with a three-valve bypass so they can be serviced without stopping production. After any service, make sure the bypass valve is closed and the inlet and outlet valves are open; an open bypass sends wet air straight past the dryer.
