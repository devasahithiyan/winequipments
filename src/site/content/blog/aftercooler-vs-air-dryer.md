An aftercooler and an air dryer both take water out of compressed air, so they are often confused or treated as alternatives. They are not. They do different jobs at different points in the line, and most plants need both.

## The short answer

- An **aftercooler** cools hot air straight out of the compressor. Most of the water vapour condenses, and a moisture separator drains it. The air leaves still saturated, only cooler.
- A **refrigerated air dryer** cools that air further, to a **+3 °C pressure dew point**, drains the extra condensate and re-heats the air. Liquid water cannot form downstream as long as the pipes stay above +3 °C.

The aftercooler removes the bulk of the water. The dryer sets the dew point.

## Why hot air carries so much water

Compressed air leaving the compressor is hot and carries the moisture drawn in with the intake air as vapour. How much vapour air can hold depends on its temperature. At 7 bar g, saturated air at 45 °C holds about 7.5 g of water per kg of air; at +3 °C it can hold only about 0.6 g/kg (calculated values).

Every degree the air cools, it has to drop some of that vapour as liquid. The question is where that happens: in a separator and a drain, or in your pipes and machines.

## How much each one removes

The WRD catalogue gives a worked example for **4000 CFM** of compressed air:

| Stage | Water in the air (litres/day) | Removed here (litres/day) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambient air into the compressor | 5500 | |
| Leaving the aftercooler | 1280 | 4220 |
| Leaving the refrigerated dryer (vapour) | 100 | 1180 |

The aftercooler takes out the bulk, about 4220 litres a day in this example. But the air leaving it is still saturated, and about 1180 litres a day would still condense in the network without a dryer.

## Why the dryer works better after an aftercooler

A WRD dryer accepts inlet air from 10 to 60 °C, and its capacity depends on that inlet temperature. The catalogue's inlet correction factor is 1.0 at 45 °C, falls to 0.85 at 50 °C and 0.7 at 60 °C, and rises to 1.2 at 30 °C. A dryer fed 60 °C air can handle only about 70% of the flow it handles at 45 °C.

So an aftercooler does two things for the dryer: it takes away most of the water before it arrives, and it delivers cooler air, which lets a given dryer model do more. Without one, you may need a larger dryer, and hot air above 60 °C is outside the WRD inlet range altogether.

## Why an aftercooler alone is not enough

Air leaving an aftercooler is saturated at its outlet temperature. As it travels through the plant and cools further, in a long header, at night or in an air-conditioned room, it keeps dropping water. That is the water that rusts pipes, washes out lubrication, sticks valves and spoils paint and product. Only a dryer brings the dew point below the lowest temperature the pipework will see. ## The recommended sequence

The WRD catalogue's installation layout puts them in this order:

1. Air compressor
2. Aftercooler
3. Air receiver
4. Pre-filter
5. Refrigerated air dryer
6. Fine filters
7. Dry air receiver

Fit [automatic drain valves](/products/automatic-drain-valves.html) on the aftercooler separator, the [air receiver](/products/air-receiver-tanks.html) and the filters, so the water they collect actually leaves the system.

## Choose an aftercooler when

- Your compressor has no built-in aftercooler, or discharges very hot air
- Discharge air is above the dryer's 60 °C inlet limit
- You want to cut the water load and inlet temperature on the dryer
- Some uses only need cooler air with bulk water removed

## Choose a refrigerated dryer when

- Air reaches machines, valves, cylinders, paint or product
- You need a stated dew point (+3 °C PDP)
- Pipework runs long or passes through cooler areas

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an air dryer if I have an aftercooler?
Usually yes. Air leaving an aftercooler is saturated at its outlet temperature, so it keeps dropping water as it cools further in the plant. Only a dryer brings the dew point below the lowest temperature the pipework will see.

### How much water does an aftercooler remove compared with a dryer?
In the WRD catalogue's example for 4000 CFM, the aftercooler removes about 4220 litres a day and the refrigerated dryer a further 1180 litres a day that would otherwise condense in the network.

### Why does a dryer work better after an aftercooler?
Dryer capacity depends on inlet temperature. The WRD inlet correction factor is 1.0 at 45 °C but 0.7 at 60 °C, so cooler air from an aftercooler lets a given dryer handle more flow, and air above 60 °C is outside the WRD inlet range.

### What is the correct order of aftercooler and dryer?
Air compressor, aftercooler, air receiver, pre-filter, refrigerated air dryer, fine filters, then the dry air receiver.
